\textbf{Question 1: A First Look at Denoising Models}

\textit{(a) Posterior.} Both $p(x_0)$ and $p(x_1 \mid x_0)$ are Gaussian with mean
linear in $x_0$, so $(x_0, x_1)$ is jointly Gaussian and the posterior is Gaussian
before any algebra; the work is to find its two parameters. By Bayes' rule, keeping
only the factors that involve $x_0$,
\[
  p(x_0 \mid x_1) \;\propto\; p(x_0)\, p(x_1 \mid x_0)
  \;\propto\; \exp\left\{ -\frac{x_0^2}{2\tau_0^2}
  - \frac{(x_1 - \sbeta\, x_0)^2}{2\beta} \right\}.
\]
Expanding the square and dropping $x_1^2 / (2\beta)$, which is constant in $x_0$,
the exponent becomes
\[
  -\frac{x_0^2}{2\tau_0^2}
  - \frac{(1-\beta)\,x_0^2 - 2\sbeta\, x_0 x_1}{2\beta}
  \;=\; -\frac{1}{2}
  \underbrace{\left( \frac{1}{\tau_0^2} + \frac{1-\beta}{\beta} \right)}_{\textstyle \lambda}
  x_0^2
  \;+\; \underbrace{\frac{\sbeta\, x_1}{\beta}}_{\textstyle h}\, x_0 .
\]
Completing the square, any density proportional to
$\exp\{ -\tfrac12 \lambda x_0^2 + h x_0 \}$ is $\Norm(h/\lambda,\, 1/\lambda)$.
Here $\lambda = \big(\den\big) / (\beta \tau_0^2)$, so
\[
  \frac{1}{\lambda} = \frac{\beta\,\tau_0^2}{\den},
  \qquad
  \frac{h}{\lambda} = \frac{\sbeta\, x_1}{\beta} \cdot \frac{\beta\,\tau_0^2}{\den}
  = \frac{\sbeta\,\tau_0^2\, x_1}{\den},
\]
\[
  \boxed{\;
  x_0 \mid x_1 \;\sim\; \Norm\!\left(
  \frac{\sbeta\,\tau_0^2}{\den}\, x_1,\;\;
  \frac{\beta\,\tau_0^2}{\den}
  \right). \;}
\]
\textit{Check.} Jointly $\E[x_1] = 0$, $\Var(x_1) = (1-\beta)\tau_0^2 + \beta$ by total
variance, and $\Cov(x_0, x_1) = \E[x_0 \,\E(x_1 \mid x_0)] = \sbeta\,\tau_0^2$; Gaussian
conditioning, mean $\Cov(x_0,x_1)\,x_1 / \Var(x_1)$ and variance
$\tau_0^2 - \Cov(x_0,x_1)^2 / \Var(x_1)$, reproduces both boxed expressions.

The mean is $x_1 / \sbeta$, the inversion of the deterministic scaling, times
$(1-\beta)\tau_0^2 / \big((1-\beta)\tau_0^2 + \beta\big)$, the share of $\Var(x_1)$ due
to signal rather than injected noise; the posterior therefore shrinks that inversion
toward the prior mean $0$. Its variance is free of $x_1$: an observation relocates the
posterior without changing its spread.

\textit{(b) Limits.} As $\beta \to 0$ the mean tends to $x_1$ and the variance to $0$: the
step is the identity, so $x_1$ pins down $x_0$ exactly and the posterior is a point mass.
As $\beta \to 1$ the mean tends to $0$ and the variance to $\tau_0^2$: $x_1$ is pure noise,
the likelihood is flat, and the posterior falls back on the prior $\Norm(0, \tau_0^2)$.
Since $d\sigma^2/d\beta = \tau_0^4 / \big(\den\big)^2 > 0$, the variance climbs
monotonically between these limits, so $\beta$ sets how much of $x_0$ survives the step.

\textit{(c) Graphical model.} The statement describes one $(x_0, x_1)$ pair; the plate
shows the i.i.d.\ replication over $N$ of them. Shaded $x_1^{(n)}$ is observed, $x_0^{(n)}$
is latent, and the fixed $\tau_0^2, \beta$ sit outside the plate, shared across all $n$.

\textbf{Question 2: Discrete Conjugacy}

\textit{(a) Posterior.} Write $\alpha_0 = \sum_{v=1}^V \alpha_v$ and
$B(\bm a) = \prod_{v=1}^V \Gamma(a_v) / \Gamma\big(\sum_{v=1}^V a_v\big)$, so the prior
density on the simplex $\simplex = \{\bm\pi : \pi_v \ge 0,\, \sum_v \pi_v = 1\}$ is
$p(\bm\pi) = B(\bm\alpha)^{-1} \prod_{v=1}^V \pi_v^{\alpha_v - 1}$; equivalently
$\int_{\simplex} \prod_{v=1}^V \pi_v^{a_v - 1} \, d\bm\pi = B(\bm a)$ for any $\bm a$ with
positive entries, an identity reused in part~(b). The likelihood is where the counts appear.
Writing one categorical draw as
$p(y_i \mid \bm\pi) = \pi_{y_i} = \prod_{v=1}^V \pi_v^{\mathbf{1}\{y_i = v\}}$ and
using conditional independence given $\bm\pi$,
\[
  p(y_1, \dots, y_n \mid \bm\pi)
  = \prod_{i=1}^n \prod_{v=1}^V \pi_v^{\mathbf{1}\{y_i = v\}}
  = \prod_{v=1}^V \pi_v^{\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbf{1}\{y_i = v\}}
  = \prod_{v=1}^V \pi_v^{n_v},
\]
so the data enter only through the counts $(n_1, \dots, n_V)$, a sufficient statistic,
and the likelihood is a product of powers of the $\pi_v$, matching the functional form
of the prior. That is exactly why the family is closed under conditioning. Multiplying,
\[
  p(\bm\pi \mid y_1, \dots, y_n)
  \;\propto\; \prod_{v=1}^V \pi_v^{\alpha_v - 1} \prod_{v=1}^V \pi_v^{n_v}
  \;=\; \prod_{v=1}^V \pi_v^{(\alpha_v + n_v) - 1}.
\]
The right side is the Dirichlet kernel with parameters $\alpha_v + n_v > 0$; a density is
pinned down by its kernel, so the normalizer is $B(\bm\alpha + \bm n)^{-1}$ and
\[
  \boxed{\; \bm\pi \mid y_1, \dots, y_n \;\sim\; \Dir(\alpha_1 + n_1, \,\dots,\, \alpha_V + n_V). \;}
\]
The prior parameters behave as pseudocounts: the data simply add $n_v$ to
$\alpha_v$, and the concentration grows from $\alpha_0$ to $\alpha_0 + n$.

\textit{(b) Posterior predictive.} Let $\bm\alpha' = \bm\alpha + \bm n$, so
$\alpha_0' = \sum_u \alpha_u' = \alpha_0 + n$. Given $\bm\pi$ the next draw is independent
of the previous ones, so conditioning on $\bm\pi$ and marginalizing it out,
\[
  p(y_{n+1} = v \mid y_{1:n})
  = \int_{\simplex} p(y_{n+1} = v \mid \bm\pi) \, p(\bm\pi \mid y_{1:n}) \, d\bm\pi
  = \int_{\simplex} \pi_v \, p(\bm\pi \mid y_{1:n}) \, d\bm\pi
  = \E[\pi_v \mid y_{1:n}],
\]
the posterior mean of $\pi_v$. The extra factor of $\pi_v$ raises the $v$-th exponent by
one, replacing $\bm\alpha'$ with $\bm\alpha' + \bm e_v$, so that same identity applies:
\[
  \E[\pi_v \mid y_{1:n}]
  = \frac{1}{B(\bm\alpha')} \int_{\simplex} \prod_{u=1}^V \pi_u^{(\alpha_u' + \mathbf{1}\{u = v\}) - 1} \, d\bm\pi
  = \frac{B(\bm\alpha' + \bm e_v)}{B(\bm\alpha')}
  = \frac{\Gamma(\alpha_v' + 1)}{\Gamma(\alpha_v')} \cdot \frac{\Gamma(\alpha_0')}{\Gamma(\alpha_0' + 1)}
  = \frac{\alpha_v'}{\alpha_0'},
\]
where the $u \neq v$ factors cancel and $\Gamma(z+1) = z\,\Gamma(z)$ is used twice.
Hence
\[
  \boxed{\; p(y_{n+1} = v \mid y_1, \dots, y_n) = \frac{\alpha_v + n_v}{\alpha_0 + n},
  \qquad \alpha_0 = \sum_{u=1}^V \alpha_u. \;}
\]
These sum to one over $v$, since $\sum_v (\alpha_v + n_v) = \alpha_0 + n$. Rewriting
\vspace{-1ex}
\[
  \frac{\alpha_v + n_v}{\alpha_0 + n}
  = \frac{n}{\alpha_0 + n} \cdot \frac{n_v}{n}
  \;+\; \frac{\alpha_0}{\alpha_0 + n} \cdot \frac{\alpha_v}{\alpha_0}
\]
exhibits it as a convex combination of the maximum likelihood estimate $n_v / n$ and the
prior mean $\alpha_v / \alpha_0$, with $\alpha_0$ an effective prior sample size: it tends
to the MLE as $n \to \infty$ but keeps positive mass on every category even when
$n_v = 0$. This is additive smoothing, which becomes Laplace smoothing when $\alpha_v = 1$.

\textbf{Question 3: Box's Loop}

\textit{(1) Question.} Runners describe ``the wall'' as an event -- something that
happens \emph{to} you, at a place -- and the physiology behind it is a phase
transition: glycogen runs out after 90--120 minutes and the body switches to a
slower fuel. If so, the field holds two kinds of second half, not one continuum. Is late-race deceleration generated by one continuous fatigue
distribution or by a two-regime latent process? I treat this as model comparison
with a guardrail: a second component must not merely fit better, it must
\emph{behave} like a physiological state.

\textit{(2) Data.} The BAA publishes every finisher's net (chip) time at nine
timing mats. I use the \texttt{llimllib/bostonmarathon} scrape of \texttt{baa.org}
for 2013--2014: \wallRawRows{} finishers, cumulative splits at 5K, 10K, 20K, Half,
25K, 30K, 35K, 40K, finish, plus sex, age, bib. One row is one athlete; a missing
mat is hardware, not a missing runner. (The loader also reads the 2015--2017
Kaggle release of the same feed, unreachable from this machine, so all numbers
below are 2013--2014.) Hygiene: drop wheelchair/handcycle bibs and duplicates;
read \texttt{"-"} and $0$ as missing; blank any mat breaking strict monotonicity of
cumulative time (a cross-read); repair a \emph{single} interior gap by
interpolation in distance, never a run of two, which would manufacture the smooth
late trajectory I want to detect; drop implausible paces. Cost \wallDropped{} rows,
leaving \wallN{} athletes whose four metric-defining mats are real reads.

$\log(\mathrm{Half}_2/\mathrm{Half}_1)$ is unusable at Boston: the course falls
140\,m net from Hopkinton, most of it early, then climbs the Newton Hills
(Fig.~\ref{fig:wall}a). Gradient multiplies pace, so on the log scale the course is
a common \emph{constant} -- harmless -- but its size depends on ability, since slow
runners pay more for the same hill (Fig.~\ref{fig:wall}b). So I take a late window
starting after the climb, a baseline ending before it, and subtract an
ability-conditional reference:
\[
  y_i = \log\frac{p^{\text{late}}_i}{p^{\text{base}}_i} - \hat\delta_q(a_i),
  \quad
  p^{\text{late}}_i = \tfrac{1}{10}(T_{40} - T_{30}),\;\;
  p^{\text{base}}_i = a_i = \tfrac{1}{15}(T_{25} - T_{10}),
\]
$\hat\delta_q$ being the $q{=}0.25$ conditional quantile of the raw ratio over 40
ability bins. Within a stratum this is a pure location shift, so it \emph{cannot}
create bimodality; across strata it removes the ability-driven location differences
that would manufacture it -- conservative, that is, towards the hypothesis on trial.

\textit{(3) What the data cannot do.} \emph{Survivorship}: finishers only, and an
athlete is likeliest to abandon in exactly the stretch $y$ is measured over, so
every $\pi$ below is a lower bound and the censoring is informative; 2013 makes this visible, its ``finishers'' being those past
the barricades when the race was stopped. \emph{Truncation}: qualified entry makes
this field a right tail of the recreational population. \emph{Unobserved
time-varying causes}: fuelling, cramping-versus-bonking and weather are invisible
to a split file yet all three produce the same deceleration, so splits can show a
second regime exists but not that glycogen defines it. Most valuable additions: DNF
mat records, per-athlete fuelling and heart rate, years spanning a thermal contrast.

\textit{(4) Models.} Three, hierarchical over $g = \text{sex} \times \text{year}$,
on the same $y$:
\vspace{-0.4ex}
\[
  \mathcal{M}_0:\, y_i \sim \Norm(\mu_g, \sigma_g^2);
  \qquad
  \mathcal{M}_s:\, y_i \sim \mathrm{SkewNormal}(\xi_g, \omega_g, \alpha_g);
\]
\vspace{-2.6ex}
\[
  \mathcal{M}_2:\, p(y_i \mid \theta) = (1 - \pi_g)\,\Norm(y_i \mid \mu_{0,g}, \sigma_{0,g}^2)
  + \pi_g\,\Norm(y_i \mid \mu_{1,g}, \sigma_{1,g}^2).
\]
$\mathcal{M}_s$ is what makes the comparison mean anything: it buys the same right
skew with one parameter instead of three, so if $\mathcal{M}_2$ wins only on
skewness, $\mathcal{M}_s$ wins instead. I marginalise $z_i$ out of $\mathcal{M}_2$
so NUTS sees a smooth posterior, and kill label switching structurally via
$\mu_{1,g} = \mu_{0,g} + \exp(\lambda_g)$, enforcing the ordering on every draw
while leaving $\lambda_g$ free for its hierarchical prior. With $\sim$$10^4$ athletes
per group the group priors are \emph{centred}: non-centring, the reflex choice, is
what funnels when the likelihood is this strong. Responsibilities return by
Rao-Blackwellisation,
$P(z_i{=}1\mid y_i) = \E_\theta[\pi_g\Norm_1/((1{-}\pi_g)\Norm_0 + \pi_g\Norm_1)]$.

\textit{(5) Conclusion: two components, but not the two I was promised.} PSIS-LOO

\textit{(6) Next step: promote the label to a location.} A scalar cannot say
\emph{when} deceleration began, and depletion is a claim about when. Over all nine
splits, with $d_s$ the midpoint of segment $s$, $\ell_{is}$ log pace, $\alpha_i$ the
athlete's level, $\delta_s$ a sum-to-zero course profile shared by the field,
$\kappa_i$ the change-point, $\beta_i > 0$ the fade rate and
$m_{is} = \alpha_i + \delta_s + \beta_i (d_s - \kappa_i)_+$:
\vspace{-0.4ex}
\[
  p(\ell_{i\cdot}) = (1 - \pi_g)\prod_s \Norm\big(\ell_{is} \mid \alpha_i + \delta_s,\, \sigma^2\big)
  + \pi_g \prod_s \Norm\big(\ell_{is} \mid m_{is},\, \sigma^2\big).
\]
This repairs all three defects at once: $\delta_s$ puts the topographic correction
\emph{inside} the model, $\beta_i > 0$ makes the wall directional so no negative
split can be flagged, and $\kappa_i$ makes the hypothesis falsifiable -- depletion
predicts $\kappa$ massed near 30--35\,km, and heat moving it earlier as well as
raising $\pi$. A first fit on 1,000 athletes puts the median break at
$\wallKappaMed$\,km (10--90\%: $\wallKappaLo$--$\wallKappaHi$), but its regime
indicator collapses: the wall branch nests the flat one at $\beta_i = 0$, so $\pi$
runs to one and $\kappa$ is being read off the whole field. Holding $\beta$ away
from zero, then refitting on 2015--2017 with DNF records restored, is the
experiment that would settle the question.
